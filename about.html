Research

My research concerns transparent, quantitatively validated AI systems for medical decision support. I place particular weight on reproducibility, reliability of annotation and geometry-aware modelling: a model that measures an angle on a radiograph should be judged against the variability of expert measurements, not only against a single reference label.

I work across the whole pipeline, from data acquisition and annotation protocols through modelling and validation to deployment, and I treat each of these stages as part of the scientific result rather than as supporting engineering.

Medical image analysis

Automated measurement on radiographs, including tibial plateau angle estimation in canine orthopaedics, developed with the University of Zurich.

Interpretable machine learning

Models whose decisions can be inspected and questioned; earlier work on rough sets for classification of cardiological data.

Computer vision

Image processing and augmented reality, including light-source extraction and image-based lighting.

Sports analytics

Video and timing data for the analysis of athletic and combat-sport technique, close to my coaching practice.

Teaching and mentoring

I teach software engineering and applied AI, and I expect student projects to be documented, reproducible and honest about their limitations. Recent courses include team programming, software engineering, mobile application development, AI agents in medicine, and AI systems design with API integration. I also mentor student teams preparing for AMPPZ, the Polish Collegiate Programming Contest, and supervise student research projects in computer vision.

I am looking for students, research assistants and future doctoral collaborators interested in medical imaging, computer vision or sports analytics.
